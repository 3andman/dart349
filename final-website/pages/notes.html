← Back to Desktop

Artist Statement

This project is a simulated Windows XP-style interface that explores how early digital systems shape user behaviour and emotion. It recreates familiar elements such as a login screen, desktop, and application pages, but alters them to create friction, delay, and resistance.

The visual style is based on early 2000s operating systems, using simple layouts, icons, and system-like dialogue to evoke nostalgia and familiarity. However, this familiarity is intentionally disrupted through reactive feedback from the system, especially through the character “Clippy,” who acts as a guide but often blames or discourages the user.

The project uses interaction as its main structure. The login page controls access, the desktop acts as a transitional space, and the application pages serve as endpoints that feel functional but slightly unstable. Progress is achieved through persistence rather than straightforward navigation.

Overall, the work reflects on how digital interfaces can influence behaviour not just through design, but through tone and feedback, shifting the experience from neutral interaction into something more personal, frustrating, and reflective.
